# Scientific paper authoring guide

The suggested workflow is practical rather than mandatory.

## Recommended writing order

1. Prepare figures in near-final form.
2. Draft a structured abstract.
3. Write Results in figure order.
4. Write Methods in the corresponding procedural order.
5. Write Discussion.
6. Write Introduction last.
7. Reconcile abstract, Results, figures, and conclusion.

## Abstract

Draft approximately 250 words unless the target journal specifies another limit.

### Background / Purpose

Use one or two sentences. State:

- the scientific problem;
- the method or approach;
- the principal parameters;
- the purpose or hypothesis.

### Methods

Report:

- the number of subjects, specimens, datasets, simulations, or systems;
- the major techniques;
- the principal analysis.

### Results

Use past tense. Include every number needed to support the conclusion. The paragraph
may read more like a compact table than literary prose.

### Conclusion

State the take-home message. Do not introduce a result or interpretation that does not
appear elsewhere.

## Results

- Use past tense.
- Follow the numerical order of the figures.
- Do not mention Figure 2 before Figure 1.
- Begin with completion, exclusions, failures, or dropouts.
- Report quantitative values, uncertainty, exact sample sizes, and exact test results
  where appropriate.
- Ensure every abstract result appears in the Results section in the same order.
- Avoid mixing interpretation into the result statement.

## Methods

Recommended order:

1. subjects or source population;
2. investigated materials or datasets;
3. experimental or computational procedure;
4. preprocessing;
5. outcome measures;
6. statistical analysis;
7. reproducibility and provenance.

Define replicates, error bars, one- or two-tailed tests, exact \(n\), software versions,
and exclusion rules.

## Discussion

One useful structure is:

1. overall meaning;
2. technical or conceptual novelty;
3. relation to previous literature;
4. possible reasons for differences;
5. limitations;
6. positive statement after limitations;
7. summary.

Keep it concise. Do not introduce a new result in the final paragraph.

## Introduction

- Explain the general scientific need in the first two or three sentences.
- Cite prior groups constructively.
- Define the gap.
- State the hypothesis or central question.
- Number multiple aims.
- Keep the stated purpose consistent with the abstract.
