\begin{document}
\MakeGenesisTitle
\begin{GenesisAbstract}
State the purpose, intended output, domain of use, and validation status.
\end{GenesisAbstract}
\section{Scope}
Define what the method does, what it does not do, intended users, and conditions under
which it should not be used.
\section{Safety, ethics, privacy, and rights}
Describe biological, chemical, clinical, computational, privacy, legal, or key-management
risks before the procedure begins.
\section{Inputs and prerequisites}
List materials, samples, datasets, instruments, software, accounts, permissions, network
settings, and required expertise.
\section{Parameters and configuration}
Record defaults, allowed ranges, units, versions, and environment assumptions.
\section{Procedure}
\begin{GenesisProcedure}
\begin{genesissteps}
\item Prepare and verify all inputs.
\item Record initial state, identifiers, and checksums.
\item Execute the method step by step.
\item Perform quality-control checks at defined checkpoints.
\item Store outputs and provenance records.
\end{genesissteps}
\end{GenesisProcedure}
\section{Quality control and acceptance criteria}
Specify checks, tolerances, controls, and rejection rules.
\section{Expected outputs}
Describe normal outputs, units, file formats, state changes, and interpretation limits.
\section{Failure modes and troubleshooting}
List failures, diagnostic signs, likely causes, corrective actions, and stop conditions.
\section{Validation}
State how the method was tested, by whom, against what reference, and with what result.
\begin{GenesisProvenance}
Record exact commits, containers, firmware, model IDs, contract addresses, transaction
hashes, chain ID, block height, and data commitments only when relevant.
\end{GenesisProvenance}
\begin{GenesisTrustBoundary}
Deterministic execution can show that a specified procedure ran over exact inputs. It does
not establish scientific validity, clinical safety, legal compliance, or suitability for an
untested context.
\end{GenesisTrustBoundary}
